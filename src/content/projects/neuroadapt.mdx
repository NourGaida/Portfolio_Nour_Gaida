## Overview

NeuroAdapt is a research-oriented deep learning project focused on **multimodal brain tumor segmentation** from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).

The project explores how complementary information from four MRI sequences — **T1, T1ce, T2, and FLAIR** — can be combined to produce more reliable voxel-level tumor segmentation.

Rather than treating all modalities as equally informative, NeuroAdapt investigates an adaptive fusion strategy that learns to capture modality-specific features and combine them according to their relevance.

The long-term objective is to move beyond segmentation alone by providing additional information about model confidence, modality contributions, and tumor-region measurements.

## Problem and motivation

Brain tumor segmentation is a challenging medical imaging task. Tumors can have irregular boundaries, heterogeneous tissue composition, and substantial variation in appearance across patients.

Each MRI sequence captures complementary anatomical or pathological information:

- **T1:** Provides anatomical context and structural information.
- **T1ce:** Highlights contrast-enhancing tumor components.
- **T2:** Provides information about tissue water content and abnormalities.
- **FLAIR:** Helps reveal edema and other hyperintense pathological regions.

Combining these sequences can provide a more comprehensive representation than relying on a single modality.

However, multimodal learning introduces several challenges:

- Learning complementary rather than redundant representations.
- Handling differences in intensity and image characteristics.
- Preserving spatial information during feature fusion.
- Maintaining robustness when one or more modalities are unavailable or less informative.
- Understanding the confidence and contribution of each modality in the final prediction.

**NeuroAdapt investigates these challenges through adaptive multimodal representation learning and uncertainty-aware segmentation.**

## Technical approach

The proposed architecture follows four main stages.

### 1. Multimodal MRI preprocessing

The four MRI sequences are prepared and spatially aligned to ensure that corresponding voxels represent the same anatomical locations.

The preprocessing pipeline is designed to include:

- MRI volume loading and quality inspection.
- Spatial registration and resampling.
- Intensity normalization.
- Brain-region cropping and patch extraction.
- Data augmentation to improve generalization.

### 2. Pretrained medical vision encoder

A pretrained medical imaging encoder is explored as the feature extraction backbone.

Its purpose is to learn meaningful anatomical and pathological representations from the MRI volumes while reducing the need to learn all visual features from scratch.

The initial implementation can also use a CNN-based encoder as a baseline or fallback.

### 3. Adaptive modality fusion

This is the central research component of NeuroAdapt.

Instead of simply concatenating the four modality representations, the proposed fusion module explores:

- Modality-specific feature extraction.
- Spatial attention to emphasize relevant regions.
- Reliability-aware weighting of modality features.
- Modality-availability masks to account for missing inputs.

The objective is to allow the network to adapt how information from T1, T1ce, T2, and FLAIR contributes to the final representation.

### 4. Segmentation decoder

A lightweight U-Net-style decoder uses the fused representations to generate voxel-level segmentation predictions.

The intended outputs include:

- **Whole Tumor (WT):** The complete tumor region.
- **Tumor Core (TC):** The tumor core, excluding surrounding edema.
- **Enhancing Tumor (ET):** The contrast-enhancing tumor region.

Additional explainability outputs are also being explored:

- Voxel-level uncertainty maps.
- Modality-contribution visualizations.
- Quantitative tumor-region measurements.

## Dataset and preprocessing

The project plans to use the **BraTS (Brain Tumor Segmentation) dataset**, a benchmark for multimodal brain tumor segmentation.

Each subject provides four complementary MRI sequences:

| Modality | Information |
|---|---|
| T1 | Anatomical structure |
| T1ce | Contrast-enhancing regions |
| T2 | Tissue and fluid abnormalities |
| FLAIR | Edema and hyperintense regions |

The preprocessing pipeline will ensure consistent spatial dimensions, intensity normalization, and alignment across modalities.

Data partitioning will be performed at the **patient level** to prevent slices or patches from the same subject leaking across training, validation, and test sets.

## Experiments and evaluation

The experimental plan is designed to evaluate the contribution of each architectural component independently.

| Experiment | Objective |
|---|---|
| E1 — Baseline | Establish a four-channel U-Net baseline. |
| E2 — Pretrained encoder | Evaluate pretrained feature representations. |
| E3 — Adaptive fusion | Measure the contribution of attention-based modality fusion. |
| E4 — Modality dropout | Investigate robustness to missing MRI sequences. |
| E5 — Uncertainty estimation | Analyze prediction confidence and uncertain regions. |

The evaluation will consider:

- **Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC):** Measures overlap between predicted and reference segmentation.
- **Hausdorff Distance 95 (HD95):** Evaluates boundary discrepancies.
- **Sensitivity and precision:** Assess tumor-region detection.
- **Region-wise evaluation:** Separate assessment of WT, TC, and ET.

Qualitative analysis will include MRI slices, ground-truth masks, predicted segmentation overlays, and uncertainty visualizations.

## Challenges and lessons learned

The project addresses several important research challenges:

- **Multimodal representation learning:** Combining complementary information without losing modality-specific characteristics.
- **Class imbalance:** Learning from tumor regions that occupy a relatively small portion of MRI volumes.
- **Spatial consistency:** Preserving voxel-level alignment throughout the architecture.
- **Missing modalities:** Investigating how the model behaves when an MRI sequence is unavailable.
- **Model reliability:** Distinguishing confident predictions from uncertain segmentation regions.

These challenges motivate the exploration of adaptive fusion, modality dropout, and uncertainty estimation.
